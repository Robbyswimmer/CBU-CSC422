# Research Paper Presentation — Rubric

**Total: 100 points** (10% of the course grade)

Half the weight sits on **core contribution** and **technical explanation**, because
those are the two things a listener cannot get from reading the abstract themselves.

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Paper Overview & Motivation | 15 |
| Core Contribution | 25 |
| Technical Explanation | 25 |
| Results & Critical Assessment | 15 |
| Impact & Takeaway | 10 |
| Delivery, Slides & Timing | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

---

## Paper Overview & Motivation (15 points)

| Points | Level | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **15 – Exceptional** | Framed for the room | Opens in plain language before any notation. The class understands the problem and why it was worth solving within the first two minutes. Situates the paper against what came before. |
| **12 – Proficient** | Clear framing | Problem and motivation are clear. Some context for prior work. A listener could restate the problem afterwards. |
| **9 – Developing** | Partial framing | States the problem but jumps to method too quickly, or motivation is asserted rather than explained. |
| **6 – Inadequate** | Unclear | Opens in notation or jargon. The class is lost before the method starts. |
| **0** | Absent | No framing of problem or motivation. |

## Core Contribution (25 points)

*The single hardest thing to do well: separating what is new from what is background.*

| Points | Level | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **25 – Exceptional** | Precisely identified | States the contribution in one clear sentence and defends it. Distinguishes the paper's novelty from prior work it builds on. The class knows exactly what these authors added. |
| **20 – Proficient** | Correctly identified | Contribution is identified and mostly accurate. Boundary with prior work is largely clear. |
| **15 – Developing** | Vague | Gestures at a contribution but conflates it with background, or describes what the paper *does* without saying what is *new*. |
| **10 – Inadequate** | Misidentified | Names the wrong contribution, or presents standard background as novel. |
| **0** | Absent | No attempt to identify a contribution. |

## Technical Explanation (25 points)

| Points | Level | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **25 – Exceptional** | Genuinely taught | Explains the mechanism so the class understands *how* it works, at an appropriate depth for this course. Chooses the right details and cuts the rest. Figures reproduced and explained, not just shown. |
| **20 – Proficient** | Sound | Method explained correctly with reasonable depth. Mostly followable. Minor gaps or a section rushed. |
| **15 – Developing** | Surface | Describes the method without conveying how it works. Reads slides. Notation appears without being defined. |
| **10 – Inadequate** | Confused or wrong | Technical content is incorrect, or so unclear the class gains nothing. |
| **0** | Absent | No technical content. |

## Results & Critical Assessment (15 points)

| Points | Level | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **15 – Exceptional** | Evaluated, not recited | Presents the key results *and* judges them: are the baselines fair, is the evaluation convincing, what is missing? Notes limitations the paper itself downplays. |
| **12 – Proficient** | Reported with some judgement | Key results presented accurately with some critical comment. |
| **9 – Developing** | Recited | Results reported without assessment. Takes the paper's claims at face value. |
| **6 – Inadequate** | Thin or wrong | Results misread, or reduced to "it beat the baseline." |
| **0** | Absent | No results presented. |

## Impact & Takeaway (10 points)

| Points | Level | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **10 – Exceptional** | Lands the point | Articulates why this matters for ML/AI and what changed because of it. The class leaves with a clear takeaway they could repeat to someone else. |
| **8 – Proficient** | Stated | Impact explained, if somewhat generally. |
| **6 – Developing** | Asserted | Claims importance without supporting it. |
| **4 – Inadequate** | Missing | No sense of why the work matters. |
| **0** | Absent | Not addressed. |

## Delivery, Slides & Timing (10 points)

| Points | Level | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **10 – Exceptional** | Polished | Lands within 10 minutes. Slides are legible and support the talk rather than duplicating it. Speaks to the room. Handles questions confidently, including admitting what was not understood. |
| **8 – Proficient** | Solid | Roughly on time. Slides clear. Minor reliance on notes. Answers most questions. |
| **6 – Developing** | Rough | Noticeably over or under time. Dense or hard-to-read slides. Reads directly from them. |
| **4 – Inadequate** | Unprepared | Badly over/under time, unreadable slides, or unable to engage with questions. |
| **0** | Absent | Did not present. |

---

## Notes on grading

- **Honesty is rewarded, not penalized.** Saying "I could not follow this derivation"
  scores better than bluffing through it. Bluffing that gets caught in questions costs
  points under Core Contribution and Technical Explanation.
- **Ten minutes is a constraint, not a target.** A talk that covers less but teaches it
  properly beats one that races through everything.
- **Paper choice is your responsibility.** A paper too far beyond the course will show up
  as weak scores in Technical Explanation. Ask me if you are unsure.
- Post your paper title and link on the sign-up sheet **one week before** your slot.
- Upload the **paper PDF and your slides** to Canvas by the day you present. The assignment
  is open all semester; your real deadline is your own presentation date.
