# Research Paper Presentation

**Weight:** 10% of the course grade · **Length:** 10 minutes + ~2 minutes questions
**Format:** PowerPoint (or Google Slides), presented live on a Friday
**Sign-up:** claim a Friday slot on the course sign-up sheet

## Submitting

The Canvas assignment is **open all semester**. Upload **two files by the day you present**:

1. the **paper PDF**
2. your **slides** (`.pptx`, `.pdf`, or `.key`)

You present on the Friday you claimed on the sign-up sheet, so your effective deadline is
your own slot — not the date Canvas shows. The Canvas due date is the last presentation
Friday of the term, which exists only so the gradebook has something to sort by.

## The assignment

Choose one recent research paper in machine learning or AI, read it properly, and
explain it to the class in ten minutes.

The bar is not "summarize the abstract." The bar is: **your classmates should leave
understanding what problem the authors attacked, what they actually did that was new,
and why it matters.** You are the only person in the room who has read this paper —
teach it.

## Choosing a paper

- **Recent.** Published or posted within roughly the last three years, unless you clear
  an older landmark paper with me first.
- **Real venue or preprint.** NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, CVPR, ACL, AAAI, or arXiv.
- **Something you can actually explain.** A paper you half-understand makes for a talk
  the class fully misunderstands. Pick something within reach of what we have covered.
- **Not already taken.** The sign-up sheet records paper titles — check before you claim.

Post your paper title and link on the sign-up sheet at least **one week before** your slot.

## What to cover

1. **Overview** — the problem, in plain language, before any notation.
2. **Motivation** — why did this problem need solving? What was wrong with what came before?
3. **Core contribution** — what is genuinely new here? State it in one sentence.
4. **Key technical details** — the method itself. Enough that the class understands the
   mechanism, not so much that you run out of time.
5. **Results** — what did they show, and how convincingly?
6. **Limitations and impact** — what does this not do? What changed in the field because
   of it?

## Practical notes

- **Ten minutes is short.** You cannot cover a whole paper. Choose what matters and cut
  the rest. Practice with a timer.
- Slides should carry figures from the paper — reproduce them and cite them.
- Expect questions. Knowing the paper well enough to answer is part of the grade.
- If a detail defeated you, say so honestly. "I could not follow this proof" is a fine
  thing to say and better than bluffing.
